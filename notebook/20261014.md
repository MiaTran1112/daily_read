## Brain teaser 

Imagine a hallway containing 100 lockers, numbered 1 through 100.
Initially, all lockers are closed.
There are 100 students.
- Student 1 opens every locker.
- Student 2 changes the state of every second locker (closes it if open, opens it if closed).
- Student 3 changes the state of every third locker.
- Student 4 changes the state of every fourth locker.
- This continues until student 100 changes the state of locker 100.
Question: After all 100 students have passed through, how many lockers remain open?

Break down and look at specific cases:
- For locker 12: 
    - student 1,2,3,4,6,12 will change the locker: the students # are the divisors of 12
    -> 6 times the locker is toggled and the final state is CLOSED.
- For locker 9:
    - student 1,3,9 will change the locker: the students # are the divisors of 9
    -> 3 times the locker is toggled and the final state is OPEN.

So if the number of students toggle is even -> eventual state of the locker is closed.
If the number of students toggle is odd -> eventual states of the locker is open.

- We notice:
    - locker 2: 1,2 
    - locker 3: 1,3
    - locker 4: 1,2,4
    - locker 5: 1,5
    - locker 6: 1,2,3,6
    - locker 7: 1,7
    - locker 8: 1,2,4,8
    - locker 9: 1,3,9
-> most of the lockers will have the number of students toggling is EVEN, except for some number: locker 4, 9. They are both perfect square (of 2 and 3). this raises a pattern that perfect squares locker will have an odd number of students toggle it.

- we check hypothesis:
    - locker 16: 1,2,4,8,16 -> odd
    - locker 25: 1,5,25 -> odd
    - locker 36: 1,3,4,6,9,12,36 -> odd
-> our observations seem right.

so there are locker from 1-100, the highest perfect square is 100 = 10x10, so there are 10 perfect square that is created from 1-10.
-> 10 lockers remain open.
