### Variance Explained: Understanding Bayesian A/B testing (using baseball statistics) (https://varianceexplained.org/r/bayesian_ab_baseball/)

- Bayesian A/B testing = comparing two posterior distributions. 
    - instead of only comparing observed batting averages, ask: what is the probability that one batter’s true batting average is higher than the other’s? same idea as comparing conversion/click-through rates in an A/B test.
- posterior probability: P(Piazza > Aaron) = probability a random draw from Piazza’s posterior is higher than one from Aaron’s. Simulation with rbeta() gives ~60.7%.

- 4 ways to calculate posterior probability:
    - Simulation: draw many samples from both posterior distributions and compare them
    - Numerical integration: calculate how much joint probability lies in the region where Piazza > Aaron.
    - Closed-form solution: use an exact mathematical formula when one exists.
    - Closed-form approximation: approximate the Beta distributions with Normal distributions when alpha and beta are large.

- normal approximation: when Beta alpha and beta are large, Beta ≈ Normal, making probability calculations much faster and vectorizable.
- credible interval for difference: 
    - estimate the posterior difference between A and B and its uncertainty. 
    - with little data: Bayesian intervals can be narrower because the prior shrinks estimates
    - with lots of data: they converge toward frequentist confidence intervals.
- Bayesian FDR: 
    - when comparing many players, use posterior error probabilities -> `qvalue = cummean(posterior)` to control the expected false discovery rate. qvalue < .05 gives a group with ~5% expected false discoveries.
