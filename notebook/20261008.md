## Daily Read: CDC Works, but the Numbers Are Wrong

**Article:** [Part 3 — CDC, and the two numbers that came out wrong](https://markphammm.substack.com/p/i-rebuilt-my-ingestion-pipeline-with-85c)  
**Author:** Mark Pham · September 19, 2026  
**Tags:** `cdc` `fivetran` `dbt` `data-quality`

**Core idea:** A successful pipeline can produce incorrect metrics when models ignore the landing tables’ deletion and row-identity semantics.

### 1. Incremental ingestion ≠ incremental transformation

Fivetran reads Postgres WAL changes; dbt rebuilds the demo’s tables completely. Five source-row changes produced six replicated rows.

### 2. Why one update becomes two rows

Without a source primary key, Fivetran uses a hash-based `_FIVETRAN_ID`. Changing a value creates a new identity: the new version is inserted and the old version soft-deleted. `_FIVETRAN_SYNCED` alone cannot distinguish them.

### 3. Two silent reporting errors

- **Duplicate customer:** both versions join to orders, producing two report rows with full revenue.
- **Inflated revenue:** a deleted $300 order line remains in the total because staging ignores `_FIVETRAN_DELETED`.

### 4. Essential staging fix

For current-state reporting in this setup, exclude retired rows:

```sql
WHERE COALESCE(_FIVETRAN_DELETED, FALSE) = FALSE
```

### 5. Validation lessons

Check inserts, updates, and deletes through landing, staging, and reporting. Verify expected metric changes and automatic job triggering.

**Main takeaway:** Managed infrastructure removes operational work; understanding CDC semantics and validating business numbers remain your responsibility.
